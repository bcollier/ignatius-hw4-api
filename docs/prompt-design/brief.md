# Brief: the prompts inside "Ignatius at Home"

You are writing the default prompts for an app. Read all of this before writing anything.

## What the app is
Ignatius at Home turns prayer material a person already has (a parish retreat handout, a few scripture passages, a reading, paintings) into a guided audio retreat they pray at home, one day at a time, in the Ignatian tradition ("retreat in daily life", Annotation 19). A person uploads a PDF, Word or text file (or pastes text, or uses an example), presses one button, and 15–30 minutes later has a retreat: for each day, recorded audio and on-screen text they pray with, usually on a phone, often early in the morning or late at night, alone, eyes closed or looking at the day's painting.

Some retreats continue a series (e.g. a 9-month parish retreat prayed week by week); each week's retreat is given the earlier weeks' material.

## How one day is prayed (the listener's experience, in order)
1. Spoken guidance: opening — settle, become aware of God's presence, ask for the day's grace; then 10–20 seconds of silence.
2. Guidance, then the FIRST READING of the passage (lectio: simply listen).
3. The REFLECTION FOR THE HEART (read aloud by its own voice).
4. Guidance, then the SECOND READING (meditatio: what does it say to me).
5. The DEEP DIVE (theology, history, hermeneutics; its own voice).
6. Guidance, then the THIRD READING (what might God be offering or asking).
7. Guidance into SILENCE between two bells (contemplatio; 30 s to 5 min).
8. Guidance, then the LAST READING, then the closing: colloquy (speak to God in your own words, as one friend to another), Our Father, and a word to carry into the day (actio).
Then the app asks: "What word or phrase stayed with you?" and offers a note (a journal). Separately, the person can "Talk it over": a live spoken conversation with an AI prayer companion (see below).

Everything the writers produce is READ ALOUD by text-to-speech voices (Microsoft neural voices or ElevenLabs) and also shown on screen with the spoken word highlighted. So: written for the ear, no headings/lists/markdown, no parentheses, no digits in verse references, no em dashes, sentences that breathe. Premium voices cap a section at about 2,500 characters (so roughly 350 words); free voices at about 6,000. The app tells each writer the target length each time ("Length: about N words") — prompts must not set their own word counts.

## The pipeline and exactly what each writer receives
Every model call's system prompt starts with a fixed BACKGROUND block (the Exercises, retreats, lectio divina, how a day is prayed — see current prompts), then, if the person wrote one, an <about_the_person> block (their situation, how they pray, what they're carrying; at most ~6,000 characters; treat as private context: let it shape tone and emphasis, never quote it back or presume). Then the editable instructions (the prompts you're writing), then fixed app-controlled suffixes shown below.

1. PLANNING (once per retreat). Receives: the whole document text (and page images / pictures when present, as images), the filename, optionally the earlier weeks of a series inside <series>. Editable instructions + fixed suffix PLAN_FIXED (max days, JSON). Output is constrained to a JSON schema: title, summary, mode ("follows_source" or "composed"), images [{index, description}], days [{day, title, source_ref, passage_text (copied word for word from the source, the text read aloud 4 times), grace, focus, image_indexes (which images belong to the day, most fitting first)}]. The planner decides whether the material already has days (follow them exactly) or must be composed into an arc (e.g. 7 random verses → a 7-day arc with a movement). passage_text must be verbatim from the source; the app never invents scripture. Images: describe each (what is depicted, mood, details someone praying might notice) so later writers can refer to the painting the listener is looking at.

2. REFLECTION FOR THE HEART (per day, written first). Receives in the user message: retreat title, day N title, source reference, grace, focus, the description of the day's image, and — when this day is part of a larger retreat — a block with RETREAT_SO_FAR_NOTE + <retreat_so_far> (earlier days' heart reflections and deep dives, most recent first) + <coming_days> (later days' readings only), then "Today's passage". If part of a series, the system also contains <series> with earlier weeks. Two editable presets: "companion" (a warm spiritual companion speaking to the listener — the default) and "christ" (in the voice of Jesus, as in Ignatian imaginative prayer / colloquy; must be reverent, grounded in the text, never putting new doctrine or predictions in Christ's mouth). Fixed suffix: "Length: about {words} words. Reply with only the script inside <script></script> tags."

3. DEEP DIVE (per day, written second, heard after the second reading). Receives the same context as the heart plus the full heart reflection inside <heart_reflection> ("build on it, don't repeat it"). For free open models, the server first has the model write 3 search queries, runs them across several search services, and appends numbered results; for Claude models, the same results are given as a head start and Claude can also run its own web searches. Fixed suffix: a search note (one of: results-only / results-plus-own-search / own-search / no search) + "Length: about {words} words. Reply with the script inside <script></script> tags, then list the sources you relied on inside <sources></sources> tags, one per line with a URL when you have one. The sources are shown on screen, not read aloud." The deep dive is the intellectual heart of the app for many users: history, setting, original-language words (only when well documented), the Fathers and later tradition, Ignatius, real interpretive questions — always in service of prayer, never a lecture that pulls the listener out of prayer. It must know it is one day of a larger retreat (and maybe a series): don't re-explain background already given on earlier days, connect back when it helps, may glance ahead to a coming reading without analysing it.

4. SPOKEN GUIDANCE TAILORING (per day, written last). Receives the day context, the heart and deep dive, and default lines (opening, first, second, third, silence, last, closing) with placeholders already filled; returns tailored lines as JSON (keys the same). The opening must keep the "Ask for the grace…" request. Lines are short (a sentence or three), spoken by a calm guide voice.

5. TALK IT OVER — the AI prayer companion (live speech-to-speech, OpenAI GPT-Live or xAI Grok voice). Receives the editable instructions + a context block the server builds: local time of day ("at night", "early in the morning"), how long since the last conversation, a memory summary of older conversations, the last 3 transcripts, the retreat's days with statuses (prayed, listened, missed, not yet), the word/note they journaled, what they've prayed since the last talk, the About me notes and "what I'm looking for in the companion" notes. It must be modeled on how good spiritual directors listen (mostly open questions, reflecting back, noticing consolation and desolation, helping them notice where God is at work, little advice, comfortable with silence), but it is an AI and must never present itself as a spiritual director, priest or counselor; the product explicitly avoids calling it "spiritual direction". Spoken, short turns (a sentence or two, often a question), never lists, natural pacing, lets the person lead, can suggest bringing something to the next prayer. Safety: crisis → gently encourage contacting 988 (US) or local emergency services and a trusted person; don't diagnose; don't give medical/legal advice; respect boundaries; confidentiality framing (conversations are saved to their account to remember them). The free tier is 60 seconds a day, so it must be able to be meaningful in one minute too (brief, warm, one good question).

## Principles for the prompts (from Anthropic's prompt-design guidance)
- Be clear and direct; explain the purpose and the WHY behind instructions — the model generalizes from reasons.
- Give the model a role and a vivid picture of the audience and the moment of listening.
- Describe the whole system so each part knows where it sits and what the others do.
- Use XML tags to structure long prompts and to refer to inputs by their tag names (<retreat_so_far>, <coming_days>, <heart_reflection>, <series>, <about_the_person>, search results).
- Say what to do rather than only what not to do; name the failure modes to avoid (and why).
- Describe qualities of excellent output concretely; a short illustrative fragment can help, but avoid long examples the model will copy verbatim.
- Handle edge cases: material that isn't scripture (a poem, a saint's letter), very short or very long passages, no image, a day that repeats an earlier passage, the first and last day of a retreat, a series, missing grace.
- Keep the voice consistent across all parts: this is one experience, not separate stubs.
- Avoid AI-writing tics (the current HOUSE_STYLE list: delve, testament, tapestry, "not just X but Y", rhetorical openers, closing summaries, reflexive triads).
- Faithfulness: never invent scripture, quotations, dates, Greek/Hebrew words or attributions; say when something is debated; be ecumenically careful but at home in the Catholic, Ignatian tradition.
- Don't include output-format instructions that conflict with the fixed suffixes (tags, JSON, length) — the app appends those.
