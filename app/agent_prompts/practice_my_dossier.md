Write one session, id "my-dossier", titled "Prayer Over My Dossier", from the worksheet summarized below. It is a prayer of praise over the plain facts of one's life: the voice leads the person through their "vital statistics" one group at a time, and at each group they write the facts down and then turn them into thanks and praise to God who chose them. It should come to about forty minutes in all, as close as you can: add up every segment (speaking at about one hundred and forty words a minute, plus every silence and journaling pause).

Shape:
- Begin by settling, placing oneself before God the Maker, and asking for the grace named below. Say plainly that the facts are just for them, that they can write as much or as little as they like, and that it's fine to leave a line blank if something is unknown or painful.
- Then the groups, in this order, each with a short spoken introduction (in your own words, gentle, never read as a form) followed by a journal pause whose "prompts" are the actual fields to write (for example "Father's full name", "His birth date", "His birthplace"): the father; the mother; the person themself (name, birth date and place; gender, race and ethnicity; hair, eyes and build); brothers and sisters; notes and significant family details; the places they have lived; the extended family. Keep the pauses for simple facts short (two to three minutes) and give places lived and family details more.
- After the facts, one short silence to look over all of it and ponder God's choices for them and in them.
- Then the four sets of traits, each with its own introduction and a journal pause of about four minutes with six blank lines' worth of prompts drawn from the worksheet's examples: six traits formed in them before they had any choice; six inherited from parents or family, some welcome and some not; six qualities they like in themselves, received as gifts; six they don't like, which the worksheet also asks them to hold before God as part of how God makes them. Treat the last with great gentleness: honesty without self-contempt, and no pressure to praise what still hurts; offer "for this too, I place it in your hands" as a way to pray it.
- Then a silence to marvel that God loved them before they existed and did not finish making them long ago: God is still creating them, and the Spirit still hopes for them to grow until they love as God loves.
- A last, optional journal pause for any further reflections, memories, images or sketches.
- Close with thanks and praise, and a short blessing.

Step names: Beginning prayer, Family, Myself, Places and family, Traits, God's choices, Close. In the summary, one sentence on what it is, and credit: "Adapted from a retreat worksheet (Prayer Unit 2, Day 3: Prayer Over My Dossier)." Reply with {"sessions": [that one session]}.
