Write one session, id "birth", titled "Meditation on My Birth", from the notes summarized below: a prayer of memory and imagination, in the manner of Ignatian imaginative contemplation, in which the person stands beside God their Creator as an observer at their own birth. It should come to about twenty-five minutes in all (add up every segment, speaking at about one hundred and forty words a minute, plus every silence and pause).

Shape:
- Settling: feet on the floor, slow breathing until they are somewhat at peace. Ask for the grace to be given memory or imagination to be an observer at their own birth, and to know how God loves them.
- Remembering: invite them to recall the family stories of their birth (where their mother was, whether they came early, late or on time, a long labor or a sudden one), with a silence. Say plainly and kindly that if they were never told, they can imagine; if they were adopted or never knew their birth parents, they can imagine them as they would like them to have been; and if the story of their birth is a painful one, they can stay with God's presence rather than the details, or stop at any point.
- The contemplation, led slowly in several short spoken segments with silences between, never rushed: standing with God at their side, not seeing God but knowing God near by the warmth of presence; the room and the busy people caring for their mother, who can't see them or God; the sounds of labor, and someone encouraging their mother; then the newborn they were, tiny and defenseless, taking a first breath; being washed, weighed and measured; and then, for a moment before they are handed to their mother, God stepping forward, taking them in the divine arms and gazing at them with complete love, delighted and almost playful with the joy of holding them. Then God gives the child back, looks into their present eyes, and tells them that they are God's beloved child, that God longed for the moment they would come into life, and longs even more for them to know how deeply they are loved. Put all of this in your own words.
- A long silence to stay with that moment, then speak to God from the heart: what do they want to say to the God who loves them utterly, and do they believe what God said? This is the colloquy; give it real time.
- Close the prayer with the doxology, prayed slowly together in its traditional words (Glory be to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit; as it was in the beginning, is now, and ever shall be, world without end. Amen), inviting them to bow toward God as they pray it if they're able.
- Afterwards, journaling: two or three pauses asking what they felt in the prayer; whether they can talk with Jesus about the experience, whether it felt real, and any sense of connection with the Creator; what they "saw", what they thought, and what their own longing is from this kind of prayer (and, if they were disappointed, to write that honestly too); and whether there were distractions, when, from what, and how they handled them. Say that writing is optional.
- A brief close.

Step names: Settling, Remembering, Contemplation, Colloquy, Doxology, Review of prayer, Close. In the summary, one sentence on what it is, and credit: "Adapted from a meditation by Dr. Eileen C. Burke-Sullivan, Creighton University." Reply with {"sessions": [that one session]}.
